The build planner is the theorycrafting bench at full depth: where the skill calculator models your tree, the planner composes the whole character — skills, stigmas, gear stats and set bonuses assembled into a complete loadout you can evaluate, compare and share before a single hour of grinding commits it. This page covers the planner's practice: what it models, the theorycrafting method the community's number-runners use, and how a planned build becomes an in-game reality through the game's actual acquisition clocks.

## What the Build Planner Models

The planner's scope is the complete character sheet as a function of choices. On the skills side, everything the skill calculator models — trees, points, branches — as a layer. On the equipment side, the gear catalog with its stats, the stigma system with its slots and stones, and the set bonuses that make loadout composition a puzzle rather than a list. Composed together, the planner answers the theorycrafter's actual question: not "which skill do I take" but "what does this character look like complete" — the stats it lands at, the cooldowns it runs, the combos its rotation supports, and the acquisition cost of every piece of the composition.

The evaluate-and-compare layer is where planners earn their keep: two candidate loadouts side by side, their tradeoffs quantified, the marginal value of each contested slot made visible. Aion 2's gear economy makes wrong acquisitions expensive in a currency measured in lockouts and windows — the planner exists to spend that currency on paper first.

## The Theorycrafting Method

The method behind the community's best builds, condensed into the bench workflow:

1. **Define the job before the build** — a build exists to do something (maximum sustained output, a specific encounter's survival check, PvP pressure). The job statement disciplines every choice after it; the builds that fail are the ones that optimized for nothing in particular.
2. **Compose the skeleton** — the skill core and stigma foundation that the job requires, using the calculator's discipline for the tree layer.
3. **Fill gear by marginal value** — each remaining slot filled by what it adds to the job, with the planner quantifying the tradeoffs (the stat you sacrifice versus the bonus you gain); set bonuses evaluated as compositions, not defaulted into.
4. **Stress the rotation** — walk the cooldowns and resource flow of the composed build across a realistic fight; a build whose sheet is beautiful and whose rotation binds is a spreadsheet, not a character.
5. **Price the acquisition plan** — map every piece to where it comes from (which bosses' tables, which rifts' profiles, which vendors), producing a shopping list ordered by the game's actual clocks. This is where the planner connects to the timers: the acquisition plan is a window schedule.

## From Plan to Character

The honest final step, because the planner models choices and the game sells them for time: your planned build's acquisition plan is executed against the schedule pages — the world boss whose table carries your target piece farmed across its windows, the rift whose profile includes your material staged weekly, the vendor currencies accumulated through the reset loop. The planner's output is thus not a character but a **build order** — and the players who pair a good plan with the timer discipline to execute it are the ones wearing the theorycrafter's sheet a month later. The planner makes the build knowable; the timers make it attainable; the pairing is the whole practice.

## FAQ

**Skill calculator or build planner — which do I need?** The calculator for the tree question, the planner for the character question; in practice you use the calculator inside the planner. Start with the calculator page for the lighter tool, arrive here when the tree fits and the loadout begins.

**Does the planner cover stigmas and set bonuses?** Yes — the stigma system's slots and stones and the gear catalog's set effects are composed alongside skills, which is precisely the planner's extra depth over the calculator.

**How do patch changes affect planned builds?** Trees and gear stats move with patches; versioned plans (dated saves) show exactly what a patch touched, and the community's migration notes ride the same patch cycle. Re-verify branch details against the game's current patch before committing long grinds.

**Can the planner tell me the best build?** It can show you the tradeoffs — the best build is a job statement away, and the tool quantifies rather than decides. Distrust any planner output presented as universal; the honest tool has no favorite.

**What do I do with the plan when it is done?** Execute it against the clocks — the acquisition plan maps your build to windows, tables and resets, and the timer pages turn it into a schedule. Plan, then farm the plan.

The bench is open between windows. Build the character you will wear.
