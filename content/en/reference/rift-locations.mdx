## Why Locations Are Half the Timer

The window tells you when; the location tells you where — and the rift practice lives or dies on the second half. A rift window that opens while you are three zones away is a missed rift; the schedule and the map are one tool, not two. This is why the locations reference exists as its own page: the community's staging discipline ("be in position before the window opens") has a prerequisite, and the prerequisite is knowing the position. The rift timer page carries the when; this page carries the where; the practice requires both open at once.

The map also disciplines your schedule choices: a rift that opens five minutes after another closes, across the world from it, is not a double-booking — it is a commute you cannot make. Reading the schedule against the locations (instead of either alone) is how experienced players select windows that actually chain.

## The Spawn Catalog by Zone

The rift spawn points organize by zone, and each zone's cluster carries its own staging logic. The catalog this page maintains lists every spawn point with its zone, the rifts that use it, and the practical staging notes the community has accumulated — the approach routes, the safe staging spots near each point, and the travel-time realities between clusters. The zones differ in character: some host high-traffic rifts where staging early matters most (contested content rewards the positioned), others host quieter points where the schedule's margin is more forgiving — and the catalog's notes encode which is which, because "stage early" is universal advice but its urgency is not.

Two practical reading rules for the catalog. **Spawn points are fixed; schedules live on top of them** — a point that hosts a rift today hosts it because the schedule says so, and patches rotate which rifts use which points. **Travel time is part of every window** — when the catalog says two points are far apart, it is telling you something about the schedule you can execute, not just describing the map.

## Routing: Chaining Windows Across Zones

The routing patterns the rift-running practice uses, condensed: **the cluster run** (windows that share a zone chained into one staging session — the highest reward-per-travel pattern in the practice); **the corridor** (windows in adjacent zones chained along the natural travel path, trading a little wait for a lot of reach); and **the anchor** (one high-value rift as the day's fixed point, with flexible content wrapped around it). The choice among them is a matching exercise between the schedule table and this page's map — find which pattern today's windows support, and commit to it before logging in, not after.

The routing discipline pays its largest dividend on patch days, when new rift-point assignments scramble familiar patterns: the runner who knows the map as a map re-derives working routes in a day, while the runner who memorized yesterday's specific chain is lost until the community re-publishes one. Learn geography, not itineraries.
