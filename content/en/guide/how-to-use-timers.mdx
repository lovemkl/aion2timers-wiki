## The Foundation: Know Your Reset

Every serious player's system starts from one fixed point: the daily reset. Tasks refresh, caps re-fill and the progression day begins again at the same time every day, server-anchored. Build your relationship with it deliberately: know the time cold, and decide whether you are a play-before-reset or play-after-reset person — the failure pattern is drifting across the line, doing dailies twice in one day's window and zero in the next. The reset is the metronome; set your sessions to it once and stop renegotiating it daily.

From the reset flows the week: weekly caps and lockouts stack on the daily rhythm, and the experienced pattern is front-loading — clear your weeklies early in the week so the back half is flexible, rather than discovering Sunday that the week's biggest rewards expire unclaimed. The event schedule page carries the reset calendar; the habit is simply checking it against your real calendar once a week.

## The Daily Loop

The efficient day, assembled from the timer map in the main guide, in the order that works:

1. **Dailies first, fast** — the reset tasks that anchor progression, cleared in a focused block before anything discretionary.
2. **The windowed content you chose** — the rift whose loot you are farming, the boss window your group stages for. Not every window, every day: the choice is the system.
3. **Flexible time** — story, alts, exploration, or the build-planning bench: the skill calculator and build planner live here, turning waiting time into preparation.
4. **A hard stop** — decided before login, not during. More below.

The loop's power is what it excludes: by choosing your windows in advance, the rest of the game's timers stop being obligations. A player who has decided Tuesday is the rift-farming day does not agonize at 9 PM Wednesday about a window they missed — it was never theirs.

## Staging Windows: The Group Content

World bosses and group rifts reward preparation because their windows do not. The professional pattern: the crew assembles before the window opens (five to ten minutes for spawn-in-window content), positions are assigned in advance, and the boss timer page is everyone's shared reference. For contested spawns, the early stage is not optional — the groups that materialize after the boss does are spectators. For rift content, the equivalent discipline is knowing the location in advance (the rift locations reference) so the open window is spent running the rift, not finding it.

Solo players run the same pattern in miniature: be in position before the window, know what you are there for, and leave when it is done. A solo hour staged correctly outproduces three unstaged ones.

## The Burnout Guardrails

A timer-driven game can quietly become all-consuming, and the guardrails are part of the system, not a concession to weakness. **The hard stop**: decide your session's end before you log in, and honor it — timers will always generate one more window worth staying for; that is their nature, not your obligation. **The choice budget**: commit to at most two or three recurring windows per week that are "yours" — a game that owns every window owns you. **The skip permission**: missing a window is a rounding error in a long progression; the player who quits over a missed rift farmed less than one who missed it and kept playing. And **the vacation clause**: events end, windows pass, and the game's whole reward structure is designed around returning players continuing to progress. Log off whole.

## FAQ

**How many timers should I actually track?** Your daily reset, your chosen two or three weekly windows, and the live event calendar. Everything else is reference material you check, not a schedule you carry.

**What if my play time doesn't match the good windows?** Then those windows are not yours — the game offers enough on flexible schedules that a mismatched player progresses fine. Choose windows that fit your life; the alternative is restructuring your life for a rift.

**How do groups coordinate on timers?** Shared references — the crew's channel carries the same timer pages everyone reads, staging times agreed in advance. Coordination friction is reference friction.

**Is daily-only play viable?** Yes — the reset loop is designed for exactly that player. Windowed content accelerates progression; it does not gate the core arc.

**How do I catch up after a break?** Cleanly — events and windows are recurring by design, and progression systems assume returns. Check what is live on the event schedule, re-anchor on your reset, and play. The timers reset even when you do not.

Own the clock, or it owns you — the system is the difference.
